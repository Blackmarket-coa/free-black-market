# BMC ecosystem financial analysis and monetization forecast

**As of:** November 21, 2026  
**Period:** January–December 2027, USD  
**Status:** Conditional planning scenarios, not booked revenue, an approved price list, a valuation, or verified launch readiness. No application billing, prices, commissions, entitlements or production flags are changed.

## 1. Findings and financial scope

The new plans connect FBM's game-view member portal and original creator commerce, Blackout's Coalition exploration/collection game and creator participation surfaces, and Blackmask's Chats/Spaces/Vault experience. The games share community/place context and supported content, while FBM remains the commerce authority. They create several ways to sell an original product, not several platform fees on one sale.

The strongest initial revenue opportunity is **founder-owned one-time products**, followed by repeat marketplace activity. Blackmask storage and finite services can diversify income after their costs and delivery are verified. Gameplay increases discovery and demand; XP and collection counts are not revenue.

The base scenario produces **$17,318 in annual BMC revenue** and **$7,630 in operating surplus before founder pay and taxes**. After an illustrative 40 unpaid founder hours/month at $25/hour, it produces **-$4,370**. These are assumptions applied to a prelaunch audience, not a demonstrated business run rate.

Actual orders, active members, geography, retention, available listings, current revenue, storage capacity and provider invoices were not supplied or inspected. The defensible committed forecast for every unverified stream is zero until its release and delivery conditions are met. Scenario launch months are modeling assumptions; December 31 store availability remains a target.

## 3. Revenue ownership and counting rules

| Stream | BMC revenue | Money excluded from BMC revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Third-party FBM goods, services and eligible digital sales | One retained share of the flat 3% Coalition fee, after processing and fee reversals | Seller/creator proceeds, payment-provider charges, tax and shipping pass-through |
| Founder/BMC-owned enhancements | Product selling price less refunds; payment/distribution costs are expenses | No second internal 3% revenue entry and no assumed outside creator royalty |
| Third-party original game packs | Same single FBM commission; game packs are a separate GMV category | Pack price and creator proceeds are not BMC product revenue |
| Blackmask storage | BMC's delivered monthly storage service, less refunds | Do not copy this recurring permission to FBM software or Blackout platform tiers |
| Finite BMC setup/training/service projects | BMC-contracted delivered service receipts, with fulfillment costs shown | Community members' service receipts; those qualify for a fee only under actual commerce terms |
| BMC-managed finite sponsor campaign | A proposed explicit fixed delivery fee | Creator compensation, prize inventory and restricted campaign funds |
| FBM hosted/external resource use | At-cost pass-through under charter; no modeled profit | No invented hosting markup |
| Tips, fundraising, grants, affiliates, hardware, item transfers | Zero in these scenarios pending terms, demand and delivery | Recipient tips, restricted donations, unfunded rewards, game currency and unsold inventory |

Several apps can reference one order, but order ID, seller, deliverable and settlement remain canonical. Count a person once in ecosystem active members. Attribution to Blackout/Blackmask describes origin, not new revenue. A transfer does not imply a new sale, royalty or fee. Creator membership recurrence remains unresolved in Blackout and excluded. Blackmask's standalone add-on commission remains unset; its modeled third-party sales use FBM's existing 3% contract only.

Existing founder-created enhancements are treated as first-party. Future creator products and collaborations need their own ownership/split terms. Community revenue allocations are not yet established: scenarios assume BMC retains 100% of the 3% fee and include a 75% retention sensitivity. The 3% itself never increases.

Month 1 is January 2027; month 13 disables a revenue stream for this forecast. The editable input file records source notes for each driver. No paid enhancement, storage price, sponsor fee, new commission, contract or release date is approved by this analysis.

Core calculations:

- Initial ecosystem actives = social followers × registration conversion × activation.
- Subsequent actives = previous month actives × (1 + net active-member growth).
- Third-party GMV = actives × category buyer share × monthly orders/buyer × average order value, only after the assumed release month.
- Gross Coalition fee = 3% × (third-party GMV − applicable processing/distribution cost). Reverse the fee proportionally for assumed full refunds; retained fee applies the BMC retention share.
- Seller settlement = GMV − refunds − processing/distribution cost − gross Coalition fee after reversals. Nonrefunded original processing fees remain allocated to the seller in this model.
- First-party units at launch = current active cohort × initial paid conversion. Later units = net-new actives × initial conversion + continuing actives × distinct-product purchase rate.
- First-party revenue = units × allocated product prices × (1 − refunds). Processor/distribution costs are an expense. No internal platform-fee revenue is added.
- Storage revenue = active members × paid-storage share × monthly price × (1 − refunds), with incremental storage and processing expenses.
- Operating surplus = retained fees + first-party/storage/finite-service/campaign revenue − owned-sale processing − direct fulfillment − payout costs − losses − incremental operations − fixed expenses.

Revenue assumes products/services are delivered in the modeled month. Annual prepaid storage, future-service prepayments, tax, payment timing, working capital and deferred revenue require separate schedules if introduced. Peak funding is the maximum cumulative modeled operating deficit with no opening cash; it is not a complete capital requirement or a recommended cash reserve.

## 6. Payments, native distribution and small-order economics

[Stripe US standard card pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing) is 2.9% + $0.30 per successful domestic-card transaction at review. Actual payment method, country, negotiated terms, disputes and nonreturned fees can change costs.

The conservative Connect expense uses the [You-handle-pricing example](https://stripe.com/connect/pricing): $2 per monthly active payout account plus 0.25% + $0.25 per payout, with one payout/account/month. Other configurations differ. These charges are modeled as BMC expenses until the actual configuration and cost bearer are established; do not automatically add them to seller fees.

Digital sales assume 25% native billing at an illustrative 15% distribution cost and 75% web cards. [Apple's Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/) requires eligibility/enrollment for its 15% rate. [Google's published fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en) depend on program, market and, under its regional rollout, install history and billing route. Neither a universal 15% rate nor fee-free external linking is assumed as policy. The 30% case is a sensitivity, not a prediction.

For third-party native digital sales, this scenario allocates the external channel cost before the 3% split; **the exact allowed billing route, fee ordering, seller disclosure and accounting treatment still require verification before release**. This models economics, not a change to live contracts. A native charge replaces the modeled web-card charge on that transaction; it is not added on top of web processing.

Illustration before refunds, Connect and support:

| $10 sale | BMC proceeds/contribution before other costs |
|---|---:|
| Third-party product on web card: 3% × ($10 − $0.59) | $0.2823 fee |
| BMC-owned product on web card: $10 − $0.59 | $9.41 |
| BMC-owned product at 15% native distribution | $8.50 |
| Third-party native product, channel charge deducted first | $0.255 fee |
| Third-party native product if BMC instead absorbs the 15% outside its 3% fee | Negative; do not promise this subsidy |

A $2 active-account cost alone requires roughly $69.30 monthly seller GMV at a $35 web-card basket just to cover that account cost from the fee, before payout/support/loss costs. Consolidating payouts where permitted and verifying the Connect configuration matter to the fixed 3% model.

## 7. Sensitivities, break-even and budget

| Base-case change | Annual BMC revenue | Operating surplus before founder pay/tax |
|---|---:|---:|
| Base | $17,318 | $7,630 |
| No active-member growth | $11,519 | $3,648 |
| First-party products unavailable | $10,792 | $1,585 |
| BMC services and campaigns unavailable | $13,171 | $5,485 |
| BMC retains 75% of the commission | $16,043 | $6,355 |
| Native digital fee 30% | $17,302 | $7,303 |
| Fixed expenses rise to $1,000/month | $17,318 | -$1,370 |

At a $35 web-card basket, the fee before refunds/additional costs is $1.01055/order, or 2.8873% of GMV. To cover only $250 in fixed costs needs about $8,659 monthly GMV; $1,000 needs about $34,635. These are fee-only arithmetic thresholds, not full operating break-even. The charter's $8,334/$33,334 examples refer instead to the sale base **after processing**.

A $25 first-party web product contributes $23.975 before refunds/support. Eleven such sales cover $250 of fixed costs on that simplified basis; 42 cover $1,000. One-time purchases cannot be treated as renewable income from the same owned product.

Base December modeled expenses reach $1,028, slightly above the $1,000 founder-funded ceiling. Upside expenses reach $5,803. Receipts may support reinvestment, but the model does not authorize spending, assume payout funds are available to BMC, or establish that staff/support capacity exists. Define explicit operating limits before enabling costly video, media delivery, AR and broad storage.

The model includes a general per-active-member cost allowance, not a verified infrastructure quote or a media-minute/GB schedule. Capacity constraints, macOS build/signing costs, professional support, software maintenance, unrecoverable disputes and taxes can exceed it. The $250 baseline already includes current tooling; avoid counting Claude and existing services again. The illustrative founder labor adjustment subtracts $12,000/year and is separate from cash expenses.

## 8. Delivery order and evidence before revenue

1. Verify one first-party enhancement end to end: actual working capability, rights, one-time checkout, restoration, refund and net receipts.
2. Verify ordinary local sale/fulfillment and the processing-first 3% settlement. Recruit actual useful offerings and measure repeat orders.
3. Complete one creator download path: persistent draft, actual uploaded bytes, compatible entitlement/file, purchase-library reopening and actual seller settlement.
4. Complete one original Coalition encounter and collection, then a tested creator pack before turning on paid game-pack assumptions.
5. Validate Blackmask quotas, real costs, billing, restore, cancellation and export before storage sales.
6. Sell finite BMC services only within delivery capacity. Community-delivered roles remain third-party earnings. Sell sponsor campaigns only with explicit deliverables and funded participant budgets.

For each stream record: responsible owner, released version, price/fee terms, actual completed orders, refunds, paid versus earned entitlements, buyer receipt, seller settlement, and direct cost. Keep unready purchases unavailable. Never optimize sales by buying rank/votes, paywalling core encryption/recovery, or making aid depend on spending, travel or game grinding.

## 9. Replace assumptions with operating data

Track weekly unique ecosystem actives, first useful actions, paying customers by distinct product, repeat eligible purchases, category GMV, refunded amounts, exact provider charges, retained fees, first-party receipts, storage service margin, successful content delivery, creator net earnings and support workload. Use aggregate/transaction data; private messages, vault contents and precise movement histories are unnecessary.

Calibrate after 30/60/90 days. Registration, active use and purchasing require separate measurements. Track actual local supply and matching by area; a dispersed social audience does not establish dense local markets. Revenue forecasts should then use observed cohorts, product inventory and capacity rather than extrapolating follower count.

node docs/finance/project-revenue.mjs
